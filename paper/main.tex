\begin{abstract}
Spectral conditioning is standard in graph generation, but in existing generators the truncated
spectrum \emph{is} the representation being generated, which ties ``which frequency band helps''
to ``how much of the graph that representation already carries.'' We separate the two: we diffuse
the full adjacency matrix and supply the truncated normalized-Laplacian spectrum only as a
\emph{side channel}, so band and cutoff $k$ are the only quantities that vary across arms, and we add a model-free probe measuring how much of the target each condition gives away. This is an exploratory study --- one benchmark, one protocol --- and the headline arms are
\emph{oracle-conditioned}: the spectra are taken from the real evaluation graphs, so those arms are
a diagnostic upper bound rather than a generative model. Replacing the oracle with a learned
spectral prior costs most of the benefit (Ratio $5.92 \to 159.72$ for \texttt{high} $k{=}32$,
against an Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi reference of $161.67$, with \texttt{low} $k{=}8$ failing its gate
outright). On SPECTRE's Planar benchmark, a sweep of five conditioning arms over five cutoffs (one
run per cell) shows no consistent monotonic trend across the tested cutoffs: 23 of 25 cells sit
inside the spread of the unconditioned baseline, and the
effect is confined to four cells, measured against the unconditioned arm's own three-seed
spread, with two large ones dominating --- and the probe finds that both of those leak.
\texttt{low} $k{=}8$ improves the MMD Ratio $2.3\times$ while recovering $72.7\%$ of the target's
edges; \texttt{high} $k{=}32$ improves it $29.7\times$ while recovering $98.5\%$, near enough to a
near-oracle regime to be read as one. Across all twelve saved sample sets, both conditioned bands
retrieve their own conditioning graph first in all 32 cases in every seed, where the unconditioned
control sits at chance, so the advantage is at least partly reconstruction of the evaluation set. We
therefore cannot attribute either minimum to frequency content. What we can still exclude is
recovery as a \emph{predictor} of quality: within the low band it peaks at $k{=}16$, one cutoff
past the minimum, and falls $30.9$ points by $k{=}32$, so it does not locate the minimum it now
implicates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Topology-only graph generation learns a distribution over adjacency matrices with no node or edge
features, so connectivity is the only thing modelled. Diffusion models now
dominate the area \citep{niu2020edpgnn,jo2022gdss,vignac2023digress}, and a recurring idea is to
hand the generator the graph's Laplacian spectrum. \citet{martinkus2022spectre} generate
eigenpairs with a GAN and decode an adjacency matrix from them; \citet{minello2025ggsd} diffuse
the spectrum itself; \citet{vignac2023digress} feed Laplacian eigenfeatures to their denoiser as
fixed extra inputs; and \citet{zhu2025sdmg} argue, for representation learning, that the
low-frequency part of the spectrum carries the useful global information.

These motivate an obvious question --- \emph{which} part of the spectrum helps generation? ---
that existing designs cannot answer cleanly. When the truncated spectrum $(\lambda_k,
U_k)$ is the representation the model generates and decodes from, $k$ controls the frequency
content of the condition \emph{and}, simultaneously, how much of the target that representation
determines. On $n{=}64$ planar graphs $U_k \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_k) U_k^\top$ at $k{=}32$ is close
to the Laplacian itself, so a band that ``wins'' there may simply be inverting its own condition.
No cited work reports a condition-information floor against which its frequency curve could be
read.

\paragraph{Research question.} \emph{Given a diffusion model whose state already carries the full
graph, does an explicit spectral side channel improve generation, and does the incremental benefit
depend on which frequency band and how many eigenpairs are supplied?}

Phrasing it as a side channel makes the question falsifiable. Every arm diffuses the same object
with the same architecture and budget and receives a condition tensor of the same shape; only the
\emph{selection} of eigenpairs differs. A \texttt{none} arm gives the null, and three controls ---
a random band, shape-matched Gaussian noise, and another graph's real low band --- separate
frequency-specific information from conditioning capacity and from generic spectral statistics. We
then add the measurement the literature is missing: a model-free probe that reconstructs edges
from the condition alone.\footnote{Code, run artifacts and the figure-regeneration script:
\url{https://github.com/TamaraBluzer/Frequency_aware_diffusion-}. Running
\texttt{scripts/make\_paper\_figures.py} rebuilds the figure from \texttt{results/*.json}; it
contains no hand-typed number.}

Our contributions are that design; the central negative result that there is \textbf{no consistent
monotonic trend across the tested cutoffs} (\S\ref{sec:sweep}); a leakage probe that
\textbf{overturned our own headline claim} (\S\ref{sec:leakage}) --- an earlier version of this
analysis reported the winning band as leak-free, and the corrected probe finds it the
highest-leaking arm at its own cutoff, so the confound we claimed to have ruled out stands; a diagnosis of the universal $0\%$ validity (\S\ref{sec:validity}); and two
further negative results (\S\S\ref{sec:e2e}--\ref{sec:downstream}). Throughout we report
differences observed under one protocol on one benchmark; we locate an effect, we do not establish
the mechanism behind it.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{SPECTRE} \citep{martinkus2022spectre} is the closest methodological and evaluation
reference: a GAN generates $(\lambda_k, U_k)$ on a Stiefel manifold, then a graph network builds
the adjacency matrix from $U_k \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_k) U_k^\top$. It already sweeps $k \in \{2,4,8,16,32\}$ on Planar and SBM --- the grid we reuse --- but picks
one $k$ per dataset heuristically and never reports the curve. We treat $k$ and the band as the object of
study, replace its sign-canonicalization heuristic with a sign- and basis-invariant pair channel,
and diffuse adjacency rather than decoding a generated spectrum. We also offset the band by the component count, where SPECTRE drops a fixed \texttt{eigvals[1:]},
so a disconnected graph does not waste band on component indicators.

\paragraph{GGSD} \citep{minello2025ggsd} already measures smallest- versus largest-eigenpair
representations inside a diffusion generator, so frequency-band sensitivity is not itself novel.
Our contribution is narrower: the incremental value of a side channel while the diffusion state
still carries the full graph, with matched-capacity controls and a measured information floor.

\paragraph{Others.} \textbf{DiGress} \citep{vignac2023digress} supplies our datasets, discrete
diffusion process and evaluation suite; it feeds Laplacian eigenvalues to its denoiser as a fixed
input augmentation, never as a variable to ablate, and we isolate exactly that variable.
\textbf{LGD} \citep{zhou2024lgd} runs latent diffusion on molecules, never on Planar or SBM,
conditioning on scalar properties rather than spectra; \S\ref{sec:model} says why we abandoned its
design. \textbf{DualDiff} \citep{xie2026dualdiff} couples node- and cluster-level latents, but a
cluster assignment collapses global structure into one number $K$, whereas an eigenpair-indexed
condition exposes an ordered band --- which is what makes a sweep possible. \textbf{SDMG} \citep{zhu2025sdmg} shows low-frequency reconstruction beating
full-spectrum reconstruction for \emph{representation} learning; we ask whether that transfers to
\emph{generation}, and it does, but only in one narrow cell and for a reason its framing does not
predict.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Data.} SPECTRE's released Planar benchmark: 200 graphs at $n{=}64$, each a Delaunay
triangulation of uniform random points, split 128/32/40 following DiGress's recipe, which we re-process ourselves since its
\texttt{process()} appends every graph twice. Each arm generates as many graphs as the evaluation
set holds (32).
\textbf{Every number in this paper --- every Ratio, every validity fraction, every validation
loss --- is scored on the validation split}, and that same split selected the checkpoints (since
lost). No test-split number is reported anywhere. All results are therefore optimistic in the usual
way, and none of them should be read as held-out performance.

\paragraph{Spectral condition.} Let $L = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2} = U \Lambda U^\top$ with $0 =
\lambda_1 \le \dots \le \lambda_n \le 2$, and let $t$ be the number of zero eigenvalues
(equivalently, connected components). The arms take $k$ eigenpairs as \texttt{low} $=
(\lambda_{t+1} \dots \lambda_{t+k})$, \texttt{high} $= (\lambda_{n-k+1} \dots \lambda_n)$,
\texttt{random} $=$ $k$ indices drawn uniformly from $t{+}1 \dots n$, \texttt{gaussian} $=$
i.i.d.\ $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ tensors of identical shape, \texttt{shuffled} $=$ another graph's real
low band under a derangement, and \texttt{none} $=$ an all-zero condition with a presence flag.

The condition enters the denoiser twice: as a pair channel $\big(U_k \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_k)
U_k^\top\big)_{ij}$, rescaled per graph to unit RMS so \texttt{low} and \texttt{high} cannot differ in activation
scale, and as the selected eigenvalues mapped from $[0,2]$ to $[-1,1]$, supplied globally with the
timestep. The pair channel is invariant to eigenvector sign flips
and to rotations within an exactly repeated eigenspace, sidestepping the ambiguity that motivates
SignNet \citep{lim2023signnet} without depending on it.

\paragraph{Model.}
\label{sec:model}
The diffusion state is the centred adjacency matrix $x_0 = 2A - 1$. A cosine forward process
\citep{nichol2021improved} adds symmetric Gaussian noise to each undirected pair, and a permutation-equivariant DiGress transformer predicts $x_0$ from $x_t$
\citep{ho2020ddpm,vignac2023digress}, masking the diagonal and padding at every step. It has
4 layers ($d_x{=}128$, $d_e{=}64$, $d_y{=}128$, 8 heads; 1.83M parameters), $T{=}100$ steps, and
trains 200 epochs at batch 16, learning rate $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, with the condition dropped at
probability $0.1$ so guidance scale is available at sampling time \citep{ho2022cfg}. We began from an LGD-style autoencoder and abandoned it after measuring it: its per-pair latent is
an expansion, not a compression (32{,}768 floats for 2{,}016 binary decisions), and hits F1
$=1.000$ only by copying $A_{ij}$ down a private lane.

\paragraph{Deviations from our project proposal.} Two, both deliberate, both worth stating because
they change what the study measures. (i) The proposal specified \emph{latent} diffusion, an
$\epsilon_\theta(z_t, t, C_k)$ denoiser over an LGD-style autoencoder's latent. We diffuse the
adjacency matrix instead, for the reason just measured: the autoencoder we built is an expansion
rather than a compression and reaches perfect reconstruction by copying $A_{ij}$, so any latent
result would have been a result about that shortcut. Adjacency diffusion also sharpens the
side-channel question, since the diffusion state then carries the full graph in every arm. The
planned latent-transfer ablation, which would have tested whether the finding survives a genuinely
linear-size latent, was not run. (ii) The proposal's downstream task was planar-versus-SBM
classification; we replaced it with SBM community-count prediction (\S\ref{sec:downstream}),
because planar-versus-SBM is separable by mean degree alone and so cannot distinguish one
augmentation source from another.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} We reimplement the GraphRNN/GRAN/SPECTRE suite
\citep{you2018graphrnn,liao2019gran,martinkus2022spectre}: MMD \citep{gretton2012mmd} under a
total-variation Gaussian kernel over degree histograms, clustering coefficients,
normalized-Laplacian eigenvalues, 12 ab-spline wavelet scales, and 4-node orbit counts via ORCA
\citep{hocevar2014orca}. Raw MMD is uninterpretable, so we report \textbf{Ratio}: each metric
divided by the same metric between two real samples (the train-vs-test floor), averaged, so $1.0$
means indistinguishable from real data at this sample size. Validity is \texttt{networkx} planarity \emph{and} connectivity \citep{hagberg2008networkx}. We calibrated the harness before
training any model of ours: real-vs-real scores ${\approx}0$, the floor is $1.00$ by construction,
and a \emph{per-graph density-matched} Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi control \citep{erdos1959random} scores
$3035\times$ it.

\paragraph{Protocol and seed coverage.} Seed counts differ by experiment and every table states
its own. The $k{=}8$ comparison (Table~\ref{tab:k8}) is three seeds per arm at equal compute; the
$5{\times}5$ sweep (\S\ref{sec:sweep}) is \emph{a single run per cell, at seed 0}; the re-run of the
two minima (Table~\ref{tab:spikes}) is three seeds per arm; the downstream classifier
(\S\ref{sec:downstream}) is five seeds per arm; the end-to-end and discrete-diffusion runs of
\S\ref{sec:e2e} are single runs. Where three seeds exist we report a paired hierarchical bootstrap
(1{,}000 resamples) over graphs and seeds. It resamples the two levels that actually vary inside a
run, so its intervals describe how stable that particular comparison is; they are not a test over
conditioning arms, cutoffs or datasets in general.

\paragraph{Leakage probe.} The
\textbf{leakage probe} is model-free: it scores every node pair by $\big(U_k
\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_k) U_k^\top\big)_{ij}$ --- exactly the tensor the denoiser receives --- and
keeps the $m$ highest-scoring pairs, where $m$ is the target's true edge count, under whichever of
the two global sign orientations scores better. Those true counts are deliberately privileged, but
the probe is one decoder, not a bound over decoders: a \emph{high} score is conclusive --- the band
demonstrably hands the answer over --- while a \emph{low} score bounds only this
rank-and-threshold family, since a band could encode edges in a form the rule cannot read.
Gaussian conditions score at the noise floor, and the full spectrum recovers $100.0\%$, as it must.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Two labels apply to everything below. Every number is scored on the Planar \emph{validation} split,
the same split that selected the checkpoints. And the arms of Tables~\ref{tab:k8}
and~\ref{tab:spikes} and of Figure~\ref{fig:sweep} are \emph{oracle-conditioned}: each sample is
conditioned on the spectrum of a real validation graph, which makes them a diagnostic upper bound
rather than a generative model. \S\ref{sec:e2e} removes the oracle and reports what that costs.

\subsection{Conditioning helps at $k{=}8$}
Table~\ref{tab:k8} is the kill gate we set before anything downstream, and the first place numbers
appear: three seeds per arm, oracle spectra, validation split. Low-frequency conditioning
halves the Ratio and cuts validation loss by 60\%, while high and random conditioning land where
\texttt{none} lands. Paired hierarchical bootstrap 95\% intervals for
\texttt{low} minus \texttt{none}, \texttt{high} and \texttt{random} are $[-189.4, -152.1]$,
$[-206.7, -152.4]$ and $[-195.1, -142.2]$, none of them containing zero. That resampling runs over
graphs and seeds within this protocol, so the intervals say how stable this particular comparison
is; they do not license a claim about conditioning in general. The model
demonstrably uses the condition: under identical diffusion noise, adding it changes $12.95\%$ of
edge decisions.

\label{sec:sweep}

SDMG's intuition implies quality should vary smoothly with how much of which end of the spectrum
is supplied. In this sweep it does not (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}(a)). Each cell is a single run at
seed 0, so the sweep describes 25 configurations rather than estimating any one of them; the one
replicated quantity we can measure a cell against is the \texttt{none} arm's own three-seed
spread, $330.1 \pm 7.5$. Read that way, \textbf{four cells separate from baseline by more than
$3$ standard deviations, and all fifteen control cells stay within $1.9$}. Two of the four are
large --- \texttt{high}, $k{=}32$ at $10.5$ ($42$ sd below) and \texttt{low}, $k{=}8$ at $116.3$
($28$ sd) --- and two are modest and sit one cutoff away from them: \texttt{low}, $k{=}16$ at
$270.5$ ($7.9$ sd) and \texttt{high}, $k{=}16$ at $281.2$ ($6.5$ sd). Everything else, conditioned
or not, lands on the baseline. So the surface is not two isolated spikes on a flat field: it is
two large effects, each with a smaller one beside it, and a control set that never moves. We
report the earlier framing's spread for continuity --- the 21 non-separating cells span $316.4$ to
$347.9$ --- but that spread runs \emph{across different configurations} and is not a sampling
distribution, so we do not quote distances from it.

Both minima reproduce across three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:spikes}): \texttt{high}, $k{=}32$ is very
stable (std $0.58$ on a mean of $11.11$) and the only arm in the sweep that produced a valid
planar graph. \texttt{low}, $k{=}8$ is noisier but never approaches baseline; its seed-0
draw of $116.27$ was its most favourable, so $141.91$ is the number to quote. The controls do
their job: \texttt{gaussian} spans $322$--$333$ and \texttt{shuffled} --- another graph's real low
band --- spans $329$--$342$, both flat on the baseline at every $k$. Neither \emph{having a
condition} nor \emph{having real spectral statistics} suffices; the effect needs the matching
graph's own spectrum at a particular band and cutoff, which is the cleanest evidence we have
\emph{against} an extra-capacity explanation. It does not tell us what the matching spectrum
supplies at those two cells, and we do not claim to know.

An obvious objection to Table~\ref{tab:k8}: each sample is conditioned on a validation graph's
spectrum and scored against those same graphs, so a band that hands over more of the target wins
for reasons unrelated to frequency. Table~\ref{tab:leakage} measures it, and the measurement goes
against us. At $k{=}8$ the ordering is \texttt{low} $72.7\% >$ \texttt{high} $49.3\% >$
\texttt{random} $19.2\%$, with both noise controls at chance: the best-performing arm is also
the highest-leaking one. An earlier version of this analysis reported \texttt{low} at $0.0\%$ and
concluded that leakage was \emph{anti}-correlated with quality; that was an artifact of a defect in
the probe (\S\ref{sec:discussion}), and we withdraw it. The objection stands as a live confound,
and nothing we ran excludes it.

What Table~\ref{tab:leakage} does exclude is leakage as a \emph{locator} of the two minima
(Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}(b)): recovery climbs monotonically with $k$ in the high band but peaks at
$k{=}16$ in the low band and falls $30.9$ points by $k{=}32$, while quality is flat
except at four cells. Low-band recovery peaks not at the minimum but at $k{=}16$, which
recovers more ($78.7\%$ against $72.7\%$) and generates $2.3\times$ worse ($270.5$ against
$116.3$); in the high band, $76.7\%$ recovery at $k{=}16$ yields only a $6.5$ sd effect at $281.2$
while $98.5\%$ at $k{=}32$ collapses to $10.5$. Recovery is therefore not sufficient to
predict quality, and reading the $k{=}8$ minimum purely as leakage does not fit the shape of the
data. That is the honest residual: a confound we can no longer exclude, which nevertheless fails to
explain where the minima are. This defence is Planar-only: it rests on the non-monotone low-band
peak at $k{=}16$, and we have no SBM generation sweep to pair against the SBM leakage grid.

The two minima do still differ in degree. At $98.5\%$ recovery \texttt{high}, $k{=}32$ is close to
being handed the target outright, so its $29.7\times$ improvement should not be reported as a
frequency effect on any reading; \texttt{low}, $k{=}8$ leaks substantially less, but less is not
none. What replicates on SBM is the leakage itself: both bands run far above the $9.4\%$ chance
level, the low band rising monotonically in $k$ ($33.8\%$ at $k{=}2$ to $74.4\%$ at $k{=}32$)
against \texttt{high}'s $23.0\%$ to $87.7\%$. So the confound is not an artifact of Planar's
construction from 2D point sets --- but the Planar low band's non-monotone shape is not reproduced
there, which is the second reason the paragraph above is a one-dataset argument.

\paragraph{The samples themselves reconstruct their conditioning graph.} The probe bounds what the
condition \emph{carries}; it does not say what the model \emph{did} with it. We can ask directly,
by comparing each generated graph against the validation graph whose spectrum conditioned it,
across all twelve saved sample sets (four bands $\times$ three seeds, all at $k{=}8$). The
conditioned arms copy, and the controls do not. \texttt{low} reaches matched edge $F_1$ of
$0.613$--$0.622$ against $0.088$ for mismatched pairs, and \texttt{high} $0.332$--$0.342$ against
$0.086$; both retrieve their own conditioning graph \emph{first in all 32 cases, in every seed},
against a chance rate of $3.1\%$. The \texttt{none} arm --- same pairing, same code, an all-zero
condition --- sits exactly at chance, with matched $F_1$ of $0.082$--$0.090$ indistinguishable from
its mismatched $0.084$--$0.087$ and mean retrieval rank $15.3$--$17.5$ of $32$ against an expected
$16.5$. \texttt{random} lands between, at $19$--$22\%$ top-1. So the copying is caused by the
condition rather than by the pairing, and its magnitude tracks the band's measured leakage:
\texttt{low} copies roughly twice as much as \texttt{high} at this cutoff, just as it recovers
$72.7\%$ against $49.3\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:leakage}. Each conditioned sample is individually
identifiable as a partial copy of the specific held-out graph it was conditioned on, and that graph
is in the reference set the sample is then scored against. This is direct evidence for the
confound, not merely an inability to exclude it.

Two caveats bound it. The sample files are not committed --- \texttt{results/*.pt} is
\texttt{gitignored} --- and a second copy of \texttt{low}, $k{=}8$, seed 0 gives matched $F_1$
$0.653$ rather than $0.622$, so these digits are not reproducible from a clean clone even though
the conclusion replicates across every copy and seed we have. And every saved file is at $k{=}8$,
so the comparison that would settle the section --- whether $k{=}16$, which recovers more yet
generates worse, also copies more --- remains unanswerable.

\subsection{Why validity is zero everywhere}
\label{sec:validity}
Validity is \emph{connected AND planar}, so the universal $0\%$ cannot say which half failed. We
separate them two ways. On the generated graphs themselves, across the twelve saved sample sets,
connectivity is common and planarity never happens: \texttt{low}, $k{=}8$ is $65.6$--$68.8\%$
connected over its three seeds, \texttt{high} $81.2$--$93.8\%$, and the \texttt{none} and
\texttt{random} arms $87.5$--$96.9\%$, while \emph{every} arm is $0.0\%$ planar. Planarity is the
binding constraint, and conditioning makes connectivity worse rather than better. These graphs also
carry about the right number of edges --- $176.5$ on average against the target class's $177.7$ ---
so they are not merely too dense: they are the right size and the wrong shape. (We quote ranges
rather than a mean with an interval because the sample files are not committed and two copies of
the same arm disagree; see \S\ref{sec:leakage}.) The probe's reconstructions separate the two
halves the same way across the whole grid.
Connectivity is easy, and cheap in information: by $k{=}32$ every reconstruction is connected in
both bands, and even \texttt{shuffled} --- another graph's low band, at the noise floor --- is
$71.9\%$ connected at $k{=}16$ and $100\%$ at $k{=}32$. Planarity essentially never is. Near-complete
recovery does not buy it: \texttt{low} at $k{=}16$ recovers $78.7\%$ of the target's edges and
\texttt{high} at $k{=}16$ recovers $76.7\%$, and neither yields a single planar graph. Only
\texttt{high}, $k{=}32$ reaches planarity at all, at $3.1\%$.

That arm explains why. At $98.5\%$ recovery the mean number of misplaced edges is $2.7$ of
${\sim}177$ (max 6), and the fraction with \emph{zero} misplaced edges is $3.1\%$ --- the same $3.1\%$ that is planar,
so every reconstruction with even one wrong edge is non-planar. This is a property of the target
class, not of our model: a planar graph on $n{=}64$ with 177
edges sits near the $3n-6 = 186$ bound, so it is nearly maximally triangulated and almost any
added chord creates a $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$ minor. Validity here is therefore close to a binary test of
exactness rather than a graded quality measure, which is why an arm can improve Ratio $2.3\times$
while pinned at zero. The usual prescription --- add structural features --- has negative
precedent in our own runs: the discrete arm of \S\ref{sec:e2e} includes cycle counts and still
reports $0\%$ validity at every scale. We had hoped a graded \emph{distance-to-planarity} would
separate arms the boolean cannot, and report here that it does not. The measurement is greedy ---
repeatedly delete an edge of a reported Kuratowski subgraph, up to a cap of 12 --- so it is an
upper bound on the deletions a graph needs, never the minimum. At that cap it is also almost
entirely right-censored: on Planar, \emph{every} graph in 24 of the 25 cells exceeds it, and the
single exception, \texttt{high} $k{=}32$, terminates on 3 of 32 graphs, at distances $0$, $4$ and
$11$. On SBM no cell has a single terminating graph. A median over three survivors is not a
property of an arm, and the medians we previously quoted were exactly that, so we withdraw them.
The honest summary is that these reconstructions are more than 12 edge deletions from planar
essentially everywhere, which is a bound rather than a ranking; separating arms this way would
need a far larger cap and an exact solver we do not have.

\subsection{From oracle to end-to-end}
\label{sec:e2e}
Everything above is oracle-conditioned: the spectra come from held-out real graphs, making these
arms a diagnostic upper bound rather than a generative model --- SPECTRE's ``real spectra''
distinction. To close it we trained a diffusion prior over $(n, \lambda_k, U_k)$ with an orthogonality penalty
and a QR retraction at the final step, then sampled end to end with no ground-truth input. Against an ER reference of $161.67$,
oracle spectra give Ratio $5.92$ (\texttt{high}, $k{=}32$) and $74.86$ (\texttt{low}, $k{=}8$);
end to end at guidance scale 1 these become $159.72$ and $295.64$, so the low arm fails its gate
outright. Guidance is U-shaped over $\{0.5,1,1.5,2,3\}$ with an optimum at $1.5$, and even there a
$24\times$ degradation from oracle remains, because small eigenvector errors compound through $U
\mathrm{diag}(\lambda) U^\top$. These Ratios omit ORCA and are
\emph{not} comparable to the tables above, which is why the ER reference is quoted beside them.
Every configuration in this subsection is a single run, scored on the same validation split.

A discrete-diffusion arm \citep{austin2021d3pm} sharpens the caveat. At a serious budget (10
layers, 500 steps, 1{,}000 epochs) the \emph{unconditioned} baseline improves $7.7\times$ over the
same process at pilot scale, from $306.78$ to $39.60$; conditioning still helps, to $15.40$
($-61\%$), but the gap narrows from $276.7$ Ratio points to $24.2$. (Earlier drafts quoted
${\sim}327$ here, which is the \emph{continuous} baseline of Table~\ref{tab:k8}, not this
process's own pilot.) A headline from a small budget overstates conditioning's contribution, and validity stays $0\%$ at
every scale.

\subsection{Downstream utility: a null result, and a labelling flaw that limits it}
\label{sec:downstream}
The intended payoff of better topology generation is synthetic augmentation. We test it on a task
deliberately aligned with the hypothesis --- predicting an SBM graph's community count (2--5),
which is what low-frequency eigenvalues encode --- with 20/40/80 real training graphs, five seeds
per arm, and one augmented graph added per real training graph. Diffusion augmentation never beats
real-only training: at $n{=}20$ unconditioned augmentation scores $89.8\%$ against $94.8\%$
real-only and conditioned augmentation $92.0\%$; at $n{=}80$ both stay below the $97.2\%$ baseline.
Fresh samples from the \emph{true} SBM generator, our control arm, do help slightly ($98.8\%$ at
$n{=}80$), so the classifier pipeline itself works.

\paragraph{What these arms can and cannot show.} Re-reading
\texttt{scripts/downstream\_classifier.py} for this write-up, we found that the labelling procedure
does not support the comparison we first drew from it. Generated graphs are never labelled from
their own structure: each one inherits, positionally, the community count of the real template it
was sampled alongside. For the conditioned arm that is at least defensible, since the template's
spectrum is the condition. For the unconditioned arm it is not. That arm samples with
\texttt{band}${=}$\texttt{none}, an all-zero condition, so the only thing it takes from its
template is the node count. That is not nothing, and it is not the whole label either: in this
generator community sizes are drawn from $20$--$40$, so $n$ alone predicts the community count with
$85.8\%$ accuracy against a $25\%$ chance rate. The inherited label is therefore mostly, but not
entirely, recoverable from the one quantity the sample actually carries, and the residual is
noise. At the 1:1 augmentation ratio used here that is enough to degrade training without making
the arm a pure noise control, which is the reading we withdraw: the drop is a fact about a partly
broken labelling procedure, not a measurement of unconditioned sample quality. A clean version of
this experiment would label generated graphs from their own recovered structure, which we did not
do.

The conditioned arm is not a clean contrast either. It was run at \texttt{high}, $k{=}32$ --- the
high-leakage extreme of Table~\ref{tab:leakage} --- and conditioned on the real training graphs
themselves, so its samples are nearer to near-duplicates of the training set than to new
augmentation, and its inherited labels are close to correct for exactly the reason that makes the
samples redundant. The true-SBM control is the only arm whose labels are produced together with the
graph. What survives is narrow: on this task, at these sizes, under this labelling, no diffusion arm
beat real-only training, and the task is easy enough to begin with ($94.8\%$ from 20 real graphs)
that there is little headroom for augmentation of any kind. A usable version of the experiment
needs labels recovered from each generated graph --- an estimated block count, say --- rather than
inherited from a template; we did not run that, so this section does not tell us whether the
improvements of \S\ref{sec:sweep} can become downstream utility.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the side channel buys.} Very little, almost everywhere: given a diffusion state that already carries the full graph, a
truncated spectrum changed nothing we could measure in 23 of the 25 configurations we ran, and the
two exceptions differ in kind. The observation we would defend is \texttt{low}, $k{=}8$ --- a
$2.3\times$ Ratio improvement and 60\% lower validation loss under oracle conditioning on the
validation split --- and we would defend it only as an observation, because the same condition
gives a privileged probe $72.7\%$ of the target's edges, so a frequency effect and an information
effect are not separated here. Coarse global structure the model cannot otherwise infer was our
guess at what that condition supplies; leakage is now an equally live one, nothing we ran tests
either, and the observation itself rests on three seeds at one cutoff on one dataset.
\texttt{high}, $k{=}32$ is the better number and the weaker result: without the probe we would
have reported a near-oracle regime as our headline. That a controlled study can invert its own
top-line claim --- twice: once when the probe was added, and again when the probe was fixed --- is
our best argument for the information floor none of the cited work reports.

\paragraph{Two caveats on our own reporting.} First, the sharpness is unexplained: both minima sit
far below every other cell we measured, and both reproduce across three seeds, so neither is a
one-off draw --- but a mechanism predicting \emph{which} cells drop is
missing. Second, Ratio averages whichever metrics were available at run time, and orbit's tiny floor
dominates any mean it enters: \texttt{none} is $321.21$ with ORCA and $125.25$ without --- a
$2.56\times$ gap that explains an ER reference shift ($337.91$ vs.\ $161.67$) we had treated as
unexplained. Decomposing the $k{=}8$ improvement, orbit alone contributes $84.0\%$ of the $-52.9\%$ aggregate
while \emph{degree moves the wrong way}; a geometric mean puts it at $-34.8\%$. The low-band result survives --- it wins on four of five metrics and on validation loss --- but
the aggregate overstates it.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i) Every number is scored on the validation split, which also selected the
checkpoints, so nothing reported here is held-out performance; seed coverage is uneven (three seeds
for Tables~\ref{tab:k8} and~\ref{tab:spikes}, five for \S\ref{sec:downstream}, a single run for each
cell of the sweep and for every configuration in \S\ref{sec:e2e}); and the quality
sweep is Planar only --- on SBM we ran the leakage half, whose corrected pattern replicates, but
not the generation half. (ii) The sweep's values were transcribed from a recycled Colab session's stdout, so
per-metric breakdowns for those runs are lost (ORCA was verified present, so they are
five-metric). (iii) Validity is $0\%$ throughout, so we compare distributional fidelity, not
sample correctness, and our reduced discrete model is not a DiGress reproduction. (iv) SBM
validity uses a spectral-clustering stand-in. (v) Four planned ablations --- cluster conditioning,
a combinatorial Laplacian, SignNet, and a memorization audit --- were not run. (vi) The leakage
probe itself was defective when this analysis was first written: its per-graph orientation
selection never ran, so every previously reported figure was measured under a single fixed sign,
and the \texttt{low} band was reported as leak-free at $0.0\%$ recovery where it in fact recovers
$72.7\%$ at $k{=}8$. Table~\ref{tab:leakage} reports the corrected grid; the frozen pre-fix numbers
are retained in the repository alongside it, as
\texttt{condition\_leakage\_}\allowbreak\texttt{*\_single\_}\allowbreak\texttt{orientation.json}.
A second defect is uncorrected and is
larger than we first thought: the probe sizes its reconstruction from the donor's node count where
the model's conditioning path crops to the target's, so on SBM, where $n$ ranges over 55--172, all
$32$ of $32$ \texttt{shuffled} reconstructions have the wrong node count --- by up to 108 nodes.
That row's elevation above chance is an artifact of the probe rather than a property of the
condition, so the \texttt{shuffled} control is weaker on SBM than even our earlier caveat allowed
and we draw nothing from it. Planar's fixed $n{=}64$ makes the defect a no-op there. Together these make
this an exploratory study: it reports differences observed under one protocol on one benchmark, and
leaves the mechanism behind them open.

\section{Conclusion}
This is an exploratory study of one benchmark under one protocol, with oracle spectra for its
headline arms, and what it can support is correspondingly narrow.
Measured as a controlled side channel with matched-capacity controls and an information floor: no
consistent monotonic trend across the tested cutoffs, four cells that move and two that dominate
them --- both of which leak --- and an
information floor that --- once it was fixed --- refuted our own claim to have ruled leakage out,
while still failing to explain where those minima are.
