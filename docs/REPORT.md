## Headline result

**The result is a negative one: no consistent trend across the tested cutoffs.** Across 5
conditioning arms × 5 cutoffs on Planar (one run per cell), 23 of the 25 conditioned cells span
270.5–347.9 (mean 327.2, spread 18.5) — a range the unconditioned baseline of 335.7 falls
*inside*. The effect is confined to two isolated cells — `low` at k=8 and
`high` at k=32 — and neighbouring cutoffs in the same band show nothing. Whatever produces it is
a property of specific (band, k) pairs, not of "low frequency" or "high frequency" as such.
See [K_SWEEP.md](K_SWEEP.md).

An earlier version of this section said the headline was that *only high-frequency conditioning
at a sufficiently large `k` produces any valid samples at all*, and that this contradicts the
SDMG intuition that low frequencies are informative. That framing does not survive contact with
two facts, and `paper/main.tex` is right to lead with the sweep instead:

* **Validity is a near-degenerate criterion on this dataset.** `valid` means connected AND
  planar, and it is exactly 0% for every arm at every cutoff except `high` k=32 at ~3%. Ranking
  arms by validity therefore separates one cell at 3% from twenty-four at zero. As
  [ZERO_VALIDITY.md](ZERO_VALIDITY.md) shows, a Planar graph at n=64 with ~177 edges sits near
  the 3n−6 = 186 bound, so *every* reconstruction with even one misplaced edge is non-planar:
  validity here is close to a binary test of exactness, not a graded quality measure. "Produces
  valid samples" is thus a statement about which arm came closest to reproducing its target
  exactly — which is an information claim, not a frequency claim.
* **`high` k=32 is a near-oracle regime.** The model-free probe recovers 98.5% of the target's
  edges from that condition alone. It is closer to handing the model the answer than to
  selecting a frequency, which is why the paper reports its 29.7× improvement and its 98.5%
  leakage together.

So the honest reconciliation is that neither "low wins" nor "high wins" is the headline. The
sweep's flat surface is, and the probe is what keeps the two exceptions from being read as a
frequency story. **The remaining candidate for a genuine frequency effect is `low` k=8** —
which the paper leads with, and which is now itself under a live leakage confound (below).

### Correction: the leakage probe had a sign bug

The probe previously reported that `low` k=8 recovers **0.0%** of the target's edges, which was
the project's strongest evidence that the low-band result was not an information artifact.
`reconstruct_from_condition` never applied the per-graph orientation selection its docstring
promised, so every low-band cell was measured with the ranking sign inverted. Corrected, `low`
k=8 recovers **72.7%**. The 0.0% sat *below* the 8.8% chance rate, and a systematically
below-chance ranking means the signal is present with the sign flipped — it should have been
caught on that basis alone.

The external reviewer's objection ("low wins because it leaks the target") therefore **stands
as a live confound** rather than being refuted. It replicates on SBM, where `low` also leaks
heavily (42.7% at k=8, rising to 74.4% at k=32), so it is not a quirk of Planar's construction
from 2D point sets.

What keeps `low` k=8 alive is that leakage does not track quality across the band: leakage
swings from 47.9% to 78.7% across the five low cutoffs while Ratio sits on the unconditioned
baseline at four of them, and the leakiest cell (k=16, 78.7%) generates at 270.5 against k=8's
116.3. A model copying edges out of its condition would not behave that way. But this is a
correlational argument over five points in one band with a single outlier, and it is **not
sufficient to establish a mechanism.** `high` k=32's numbers are unaffected by the bug. Full
reasoning and the corrected grid in [LEAKAGE.md](LEAKAGE.md).

## Stage-by-stage results

### Stage 6 — Oracle spectral conditioning (kill gate)

Oracle spectra (real held-out eigenpairs) at k=8 confirm the denoiser can use a spectral
condition at all: low-band conditioning both trains to a lower validation loss and produces a
better Ratio than the `none` baseline, with the difference significant under paired
hierarchical bootstrap. This is the diagnostic upper bound described in
[WORKPLAN.md](../WORKPLAN.md) (M0) — real spectra, not sampled ones.

![Stage 7 pilot: Ratio by band](../results/figures/stage7_pilot_ratio_by_band.png)

*(The figure above is titled "Stage 7 pilot" because it is generated from
`frequency_pilot_planar_k8_summary.json`, which doubles as both the Stage 6 oracle check and
the k=8 slice of the Stage 7 frequency sweep — same run, same data file.)*

| band | Ratio (mean ± std) | validity |
|------|---------------------|----------|
| none | 327.2 ± 8.8 | 0% |
| low | **158.0 ± 16.2** | 0% |
| high | 339.1 ± 9.2 | 0% |
| random | 326.5 ± 11.8 | 0% |

Paired hierarchical bootstrap (1000 resamples): low beats none, high, and random with 95% CIs
excluding zero in all three comparisons (`probability_low_better` = 1.0 for each). Source:
`results/frequency_pilot_planar_k8_summary.json`.

### Stage 7 — The frequency sweep

The sweep is 5 conditioning arms × 5 `k` values plus the unconditioned baseline (26 runs) on
planar graphs, seed 0, oracle-conditioned, isolating the frequency question from the
learned-prior question. Its two minima were then re-run on seeds 1 and 2. Full write-up and
per-cell table: [K_SWEEP.md](K_SWEEP.md). Source: `results/k_sweep_planar.json`,
`results/k_sweep_planar_seedcheck.json`.

The finding is the flat surface, not either minimum: 23 of the 25 conditioned cells sit within
noise of the 335.74 baseline, and the exceptions are two isolated cells.

| band | k | Ratio | validity |
|------|---|-------|----------|
| none | — | 330.06 ± 6.16 (3 seeds) | 0% |
| low | 2, 4, 16, 32 | 270.5–321.0 | 0% |
| **low** | **8** | **141.91 ± 19.25** (3 seeds) | 0% |
| high | 2–16 | 281.2–347.9 | 0% |
| **high** | **32** | **11.11 ± 0.58** (3 seeds) | 3.1–6.25% |
| random / gaussian / shuffled | 2–32 | 322.1–344.7 | 0% |

Both minima reproduce across three seeds. `high` k=32 is extremely stable (sd 0.58 on a mean of
11.11) and is the only arm anywhere in the sweep that produced a valid planar graph. `low` k=8
is noisier but never approaches baseline; note that its widely quoted seed-0 draw of **116.27
was its most favourable** — 141.91 is the number to report, which sits close to the k=8 pilot's
157.97.

**Caveat (not auto-generated):** the Colab VM was recycled before the JSON reports were
downloaded, so Ratio and validity for these runs were transcribed from the notebook's stored
stdout and then committed to `results/`. Per-metric ratios, validation losses and validity
diagnoses for the sweep are lost. ORCA was verified present before the sweep started, so these
are 5-metric ratios on the same scale as Table 1 — see [METRIC_SCALES.md](METRIC_SCALES.md).

**The `high` k=32 cell is a near-oracle regime, not a frequency result.** The model-free probe
recovers 98.5% of the target's edges from that condition alone. Reporting its 29.7×
improvement without that number would be misleading. The `low` k=8 cell's leakage figure was
also wrong until recently — see [the correction above](#correction-the-leakage-probe-had-a-sign-bug)
and [LEAKAGE.md](LEAKAGE.md).

### Stage 8 — Learned spectral prior

Stage 6/7 use *real* held-out eigenpairs — a diagnostic upper bound, not a generative model.
Stage 8 replaces the oracle with a small diffusion model over `(n, λ_k, U_k)` itself, so
sampling is genuinely end-to-end with no ground-truth inputs.

![Stage 8: oracle vs end-to-end Ratio](../results/figures/stage8_oracle_vs_e2e.png)

| | oracle Ratio | end-to-end Ratio (gs=1.0) | ER baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| high, k=32 | **5.92** | 159.72 | 161.67 |
| low, k=8 | 74.86 | 295.64 | 161.67 |

Removing the oracle costs most of the benefit: a ~27x degradation from oracle to end-to-end for
the high/k=32 arm, which lands at 159.72 against an ER reference of 161.67 — it clears a random
graph, and barely. The low/k=8 arm fails its Ratio gate outright. High-frequency conditioning
remains the better arm in both regimes, with the caveat that its oracle number is a near-oracle
regime by the leakage probe's own measurement (98.5% edge recovery), so the oracle→end-to-end
drop is partly the cost of no longer being handed the answer.

**These Ratios are not comparable to Table 1's.** Stage 8 ran in a Colab session without ORCA,
so its Ratio averages four metrics where Table 1 averages five — a 2.56x scale difference. That
is why the ER reference is quoted beside every number here rather than compared across
sections. See [METRIC_SCALES.md](METRIC_SCALES.md).

Guidance scale sweep (high, k=32 only):

![Stage 8: guidance scale sweep](../results/figures/stage8_guidance_sweep.png)

Ratio is U-shaped in guidance scale: too little guidance (gs=0.5) fails badly (298.9), the
optimum is gs=1.5 (144.2), and it degrades again by gs=3.0 (161.2). gs=1.5 is used as the
default for Stage 9. Source: `results/stage8_results.json`.

### Stage 9 — Downstream utility

A 4-way SBM community-count classifier (2/3/4/5 communities), trained at 20/40/80 real
graphs, 5 seeds, comparing real-only training against three augmentation arms.

![Stage 9: downstream classifier accuracy](../results/figures/stage9_downstream_accuracy.png)

**Negative result:** diffusion augmentation does not beat the real-only baseline at any
training-set size. The task is too easy (>94% accuracy with just 20 real graphs) for
augmentation to show a benefit, and unconditioned-diffusion augmentation actively hurts
accuracy at n=20 (89.8% against 94.8% real-only); conditioned augmentation is no better
(92.0%). At n=80 both stay below the 97.2% baseline.

The control arm is what makes this interpretable: augmenting with fresh samples from the
**true** SBM generator does help slightly (98.8% at n=80). The pipeline therefore works, and the
failure is located in sample quality rather than in the augmentation setup. So the result says
the Ratio and validity gains of Stages 6–8 do not automatically become downstream utility on an
already-easy task — not that they never could. Source: `results/stage9_downstream.json`.

**Not run:** the optional Stage 9 stretch items listed in [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) — LG-Flow
latent transfer, DualDiff-style cluster conditioning, combinatorial-vs-normalized Laplacian
ablation, SignNet ablation, and the memorization audit — were out of scope for the committed
gate (downstream table with error bars), which is complete.

### Eval harness calibration

For scale: the training-set self-similarity floor (train vs. held-out test) has Ratio ≈ 1 by
construction, and a matched-density Erdős–Rényi baseline is ~3000x worse on this dataset.
Every Ratio number above should be read against this scale, not against zero.

## Related work

*(See [Research-Reading-Notes.md](../Research-Reading-Notes.md) for the full reading record,
including static-code-inspection findings per repository, and
[WORKPLAN.md](../WORKPLAN.md#1-positioning-how-we-differ-from-each-cited-paper) for the
paper-by-paper positioning table this section summarizes.)*

**SPECTRE** (Martinkus et al., ICML'22) is the closest methodological and evaluation
reference: a GAN that generates `(λ_k, U_k)` on a Stiefel manifold, then a PPGN builds the
adjacency matrix from the truncated spectrum. It already sweeps k ∈ {2,4,8,16,32} on
Planar/SBM — the same grid used here — but picks a single `k` per dataset by early-stopping
heuristic and never reports the full curve. This project treats `k` and frequency band as
the object of study rather than a tuned-away hyperparameter, replaces SPECTRE's
sign-canonicalization heuristic with a sign/basis-invariant SignNet encoder, and uses
diffusion in a learned latent space rather than a GAN on raw adjacency.

**LGD** (Zhou et al., NeurIPS'24) runs latent diffusion with per-node and per-pair latents
and cross-attention conditioning, evaluated on molecular datasets (QM9/MOSES/ZINC) — never
on Planar or SBM. Its conditioning pathway supports scalar properties or masked graphs, not
spectral information; the `pe` (positional-encoding) conditioning path in its public
implementation is unfinished. This project runs the Planar/SBM experiment LGD does not,
using per-node feature fusion plus global adaLN for the spectral condition instead of LGD's
cross-attention.

**DualDiff** (Xie & Pan, ICLR'26) couples two EDM branches over per-node and per-cluster
latents, where the "global" signal comes from a hard K-means/spectral-clustering partition
fused via FiLM. A cluster assignment collapses the spectrum into a single number `K`; an
eigenpair-indexed condition instead lets us sweep a continuous, ordered *band*. The plan
calls for a DualDiff-style cluster-conditioning baseline arm to show the frequency view is
not simply a re-parameterization of clustering — not yet run (see Stage 9 stretch items).
Full-text access to DualDiff itself was limited at review time (see
[Research-Reading-Notes.md](../Research-Reading-Notes.md)); its positioning above is based on
its OpenReview metadata and abstract, flagged accordingly there.

**SDMG** (Zhu et al., ICML'25) shows low-frequency reconstruction beats full-spectrum
reconstruction for *representation learning* (node/graph classification) via a multi-scale
smoothing objective. Its thesis is that high-frequency detail hurts discriminative
representations. SDMG's intuition predicts that quality should vary smoothly with how much of
which end of the spectrum is supplied; on this benchmark it does not, which is the sweep's
central negative result. The one cell that looks like a low-frequency effect (`low`, k=8)
survives as the study's remaining candidate for one, under an unresolved leakage confound; the
one cell with the best numbers (`high`, k=32) is a near-oracle regime at 98.5% edge recovery
rather than a frequency result. So this project neither confirms nor cleanly inverts SDMG in
the generation regime — it reports that the band-and-cutoff surface is mostly flat and that the
two exceptions need an information floor to interpret.

**GGSD** (ICLR'25) already measures low-versus-high spectral representations inside a
diffusion generator, which means the frequency-band sensitivity question itself is not novel
here. This project's contribution is narrower and more controlled: measuring the incremental
value of an explicit spectral *side channel* while the diffusion state continues to carry the
full graph, with matched conditioning-capacity controls (a `random`-band arm at every `k`)
that isolate frequency-specific information from the extra parameters the condition adds.

**DiGress** (Vignac et al., ICLR'23) is the codebase this project is built on: its Planar/SBM
dataset generators, discrete diffusion process, and SPECTRE-vendored evaluation suite are
reused directly (see [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md#codebase-decision)). DiGress itself uses spectral
features (eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian) as extra structural input to its denoiser, but
does not study them as a conditioning signal to sweep or ablate — they are a fixed input
augmentation, not a variable under test. This project isolates exactly that variable.

## Reproducing this report

```bash
git clone <this repo>
cd Frequency_aware_diffusion-
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/make_figures.py
```

This regenerates every figure in `results/figures/` from the JSON files already committed
under `results/` — no training or sampling required, since all reported numbers come from
finished, committed run artifacts. Training the underlying models from scratch is a separate,
much longer path; see [docs/ADJACENCY_DIFFUSION.md](ADJACENCY_DIFFUSION.md) and
`notebooks/FALD_Colab.ipynb` for that.
